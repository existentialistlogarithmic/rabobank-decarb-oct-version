T4 opex

Comments 1 and 5 - the opex delta, and how much of it the model carries

Look here when
You want to know whether the operating costs are real.
How to read it
The first block is the model's entire opex delta. Everything below it is a cost line the model does not contain. The two hydrogen rows are ALTERNATIVES - buying and self-generating are never summed.
What it means
This is the weakest part of the model and the table says so. The whole BF-BOF against H2-DRI-EAF difference is one constant driven by the hydrogen price. EAF electricity alone is EUR 89/t in Germany against EUR 47/t in Sweden - a spread comparable to the entire published opex delta, on a line the model does not have at all.

THE EITHER/OR MATTERS. Buying hydrogen and making it on site are alternatives, not additions - 55 kg/t at 55 kWh/kg is 3,025 kWh/t, the same energy the merchant price already pays for. Adding both would double-count the largest line in the table, which is the class of boundary error this whole exercise exists to catch.

The model's whole opex delta is `a`: one hydrogen row and the lumped credit. Everything marked NOT in a is a real cost line it omits.

The electricity rows are computed from this build's own Eurostat cache, so they are observations, not estimates - and they are large: EAF power alone is EUR 89/t in Germany against EUR 47/t in Sweden. That spread alone is EUR 42/t, comparable to the entire published opex delta of EUR -60/t.

The rows marked MISSING are hole H10: no single techno-economic study covering BOTH routes at ONE consistent boundary was found. Assembling them from sources with different boundaries would invent the number comment 5 asks us to stop inventing, so they stay empty.

Until H10 closes, c_avoided = 83 is an assumption, not a sum.

T5 energy

Comment 1 - is electricity cheaper than gas? Not per kWh, nowhere

Look here when
You asked whether electricity is cheaper than gas.
How to read it
Nine member states, both prices in the same unit, and the ratio. The last two columns are the hydrogen floors each price implies.
What it means
It is not - anywhere. Electricity runs 1.8x to 3.9x gas per kWh, which is the whole reason a green hydrogen floor sits above a grey one. Both columns EXCLUDE taxes and levies, and that exclusion is the gap: the tax layer is what 'fiscal harmonisation' would actually change and this build does not have it.

Both price columns EXCLUDE taxes and levies (Eurostat bands IE and I5, 2025-S2). Electricity runs 2-4x gas per kWh in every member state.
