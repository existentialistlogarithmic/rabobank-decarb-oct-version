# Calibration provenance — what is sourced, what is fitted

**Updated once the v11 specification PDF became available.** Until then the
numerical reference was Appendix A plus published industry data, and six
cost-side constants had to be researched or fitted because nothing published
them. The specification publishes them directly, and three of the researched
values were wrong. See *What the specification corrected* below; the
quantity-by-quantity comparison is generated by `analysis/reconcile.py` and
printed by `run_all.py`.

`timing_model.m` is still **not present**, so the 50-case diff remains unrun.
This file records which constants the specification determines, which were
recovered from published results, which were researched, and which remain
open. Sources are listed at the foot of this file. Nothing here is cosmetic: the whole point of
the v12 rewrite is that a number travels with its conditions.

## What the specification corrected

The v11 base-case table publishes every cost-side constant. Three of the six
that had been researched from industry data were wrong, and the error was
invisible because it cancelled at the one point anything checked.

| Constant | Was | Is | How it was caught |
|---|---|---|---|
| `K_s` | 1200 €/t capacity | **600** | §4.3: "each year of age removes €30 of residual book value", i.e. `K_s / L_s = 30` |
| `age0` | 14 yr | **8** | §4.3: the reference plant is "already eight years into a twenty year life" |
| `rho` | 0.0417 of `K_s` | **0.35** | §1 base-case table; reline cost 25–50% of book |

`1200 × (20−14)/20` and `600 × (20−8)/20` both give `S_s(2026) = 360`, which is
the only residual-value quantity the validation gate constrains. The pair was
reverse-engineered to hit it, and it does — at `t0` and nowhere else. The
reline year was 2032 instead of 2038 and the reinvestment term was €50 instead
of €210 for every year of the horizon.

**The test that settles it.** §4.3 publishes a four-row age ladder — reinvestment
year, trigger and crossing year at ages 2, 8, 14 and 20. With the specification's
values all twelve numbers reproduce exactly (2044/114.9/2031, 2038/109.5/2030,
2032/104.1/2030, 2026/98.7/2029). With the researched values they do not.

Four further corrections came from the same document:

- **The carbon price path is a list, not a line.** §1 publishes 76, 86, 96,
  104, 112 for 2026–2030. Interpolating linearly from 76 to 112 understates
  2027, 2028 and 2029 by €1, €2 and €1.
- **`f_com2026` is its own schedule, not a floor.** Eq (12) gives the proposal
  its own 2026–2033 factors, then a flat 15% tranche for 2034–2037 and nothing
  from 2038. The previous reconstruction floored the legislated path at 15%
  over 2033–2042 and produced a vintage delta of +8.97/−161 against a published
  +7.82/−122; the annex schedule gives **+7.70/−122.26**. This closes what was
  open item 7.
- **Support ends.** Eq (9) runs the Booster at full weight through 2031, tapers
  linearly to 2035 and pays nothing after. The model ran it flat to 2050, which
  made a late commitment look better funded than any published rule allows —
  and was the only reason the capex clamp ever bound.
- **Support also starts before the window.** Eq (9)'s first branch is
  `0.35 · s_max` for `t < 2028` — the Innovation Fund and national aid, €70/t.
  The model had zero there, so its step from 2027 to 2028 was €200 where the
  specification works out €130 by hand and calls it "the origin of the *waiting
  to 2028 is worth +€146* step". The model was paying the plant €70/t to wait
  one more year, in the one calculation the model exists to perform. Fixed, the
  step is €150 against the published €146; the base case is unmoved at
  2029/1,606 because the argmax was never in the affected years, which is
  exactly why nothing caught it.

**Why this one survived so long:** every other constant was checked against a
published *number*. `s(t)` is a published *schedule*, and the reconciliation
compared only the two support scenarios' outputs — both of which commit in the
Booster window, where the missing branch does not apply. `reconcile.py` now
walks eq (9) branch by branch, and two tests assert the schedule directly.

### What still does not reproduce

Every input, commitment year, trigger and crossing year reproduces. Eight
NPV **levels** run high, by 1.1% to 8.0% — 1,606 against a published 1,588 in
the base case. The residual is documented rather than tuned away: closing it by
moving an input would undo the age ladder, which is the stronger constraint.

## Tier 1 — determined by Appendix A, reproduced exactly

The validation gate (§4.3) over-determines nine constants. All nine reproduce
within 1e-3 *relative* (see note on tolerance below), with >20x margin:

| Quantity | Published | Computed | Pins |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CRF(25, 8%)` | 0.0937 | 0.0936788 | `r = 0.08`, `n = 25` |
| `beta` | 1.3860 | 1.3860009 | `mu = 0.05`, `sigma = 0.20` |
| `beta/(beta-1)` | 3.5907 | 3.5906673 | — |
| `p_M` | 27.50 | 27.500000 | `I0 = 900`, `a = -60`, `b = 1.8` |
| `MAC` | 80.17 | 80.172723 | — |
| `p*` | 98.74 | 98.743350 | — |
| `p'_M` | 30.50 | 30.500000 | `phi = 0.5`, `S_s = 360` |
| `MAC'` | 89.54 | 89.540601 | — |
| `p*'` | 109.52 | 109.515352 | — |

**Tolerance.** §4.3 states 1e-3. It is read as *relative*. The published
figures are quoted to 2 d.p. (`MAC = 80.17`, `p*' = 109.52`), so an absolute
1e-3 band is narrower than their own rounding and no implementation could
satisfy it. Every quantity clears 1e-3 relative comfortably.

Three further quantities are determined and reproduce exactly:

* `a(2.60) = -60.00` — F1's requirement that the MATLAB constant fall out of
  the endogenous form.
* `a(8.29) = -372.95` — §2.2's "-€373/t" at the Platts assessment.
* `S_s(2026) = 360.0` — the write-off behind the primed gate figures.

## Tier 2 — recovered from the published stress ladder

The ladder in §4.4 is over-determined enough to recover the *benefit* side and
the aggregate cost side, without any freedom left over.

**Trigger column — exact, no fitting.** Every rung follows from Tier 1:

| Rung | Published | Computed |
|---|---|---|
| base | 109.5 | 109.5154 |
| `kappa = 0.94` | 116.9 | 116.9203 |
| hydrogen 8.29 | 366.9 | 366.8534 |

`kappa` enters as `eps_inc_eff = kappa * eps_inc`, so `b = 1.686`. That single
structural choice reproduces both corrected triggers to 4 significant figures;
no other placement of `kappa` does.

**Benefit side — confirmed to <1 €/t.** Holding the commitment year fixed,
the ladder's own NPV steps recover the price path and horizon:

| Step | Published ΔNPV | Computed ΔNPV |
|---|---|---|
| `+ kappa = 0.94` | −128 | −127.7 |
| `+ real vs nominal` | −421 | −420.3 |

This pins: horizon to **2050**; central path **€76 (2026) rising linearly to
€112 (2030), then +5%/yr**; discounting at 8%; `w = 1`; `eps_cln = 0.1`.

**Aggregate cost side — recovered, three ways, consistently.** Writing
`X(T) = -d(T)·I'(T) + reinvestment(T)`, three independent ladder rows give:

```
row 2 (u = 0.85)          X(2030) = -334.2
row 3 (+ kappa)           X(2030) = -334.5
row 4 (+ real)            X(2030) = -335.3
row 1 (base)              X(2029) = -433.1
```

The agreement across rows 2–4 to ±0.6 €/t is the strongest evidence that the
structure is right.

## Tier 3 — sourced from the literature, not from Appendix A

`timing_model.m` was unavailable, so the six cost-side constants Appendix A
never publishes were **researched from primary sources** rather than fitted.
Five of the six are now sourced; one is calibrated.

| Constant | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `K_s` | **600** €₂₀₂₂/t capacity | v11 §1, confirmed by §4.3's €30/yr of residual. The BF-BOF installed-cost sources corroborate the order of magnitude but do not set it |
| `L_s` | 20 yr | BF campaign life between relines: 15–20 yr typical; thyssenkrupp Schwelgern ran 21 yr (1993–2014) |
| `age0` | **8** yr | v11 §4.3, "already eight years into a twenty year life". Puts `T_rel = 2038` |
| `rho` | **0.35** (`rho·K_s` = €210/t) | v11 §1; reline cost 25–50% of book. The Salzgitter €50/t figure is one plant, not the reference book value |
| `s_max` | 200 €/t capacity | Published base (Appendix A F4) — **not fitted** |
| `lam` | 0.0 | Rationing does not bind over the base-case window — **not fitted** |
| `gamma` | 0.4442 | **The one calibrated constant.** Sourced band 0.43–0.52 |

**`K_s`, `L_s` and `age0` are published, not inferred.** They were previously
inferred from `S_s(2026) = 360`, which they satisfy — but so does any pair with
`K_s(L_s − age0) = 7200`, and the inferred pair was the wrong one. This is the
cautionary tale of the whole build: a constraint that pins a *product* does not
pin its factors, and checking outputs alone will not tell you. The
reconciliation now compares inputs too.

**`gamma` is the only fitted constant**, solved from `NPV(2029) = 1588.3` with
everything else held at its sourced or published value. It lands at 0.4442,
inside the band implied by European H₂-DRI-EAF capacity going from 2.5 Mt
(2025) to 15–20 Mt (2030) — 43–52%/yr. It implies capex falling **8.2%/yr**,
which with the Wright exponent `b_ℓ = 0.234` is a ~15% learning rate.

### The `lam < 0` anomaly is resolved

The previous build fitted `lam = −0.058` — support *rising* — which
contradicted §1.5's framing of `q(t)` as rationing. That was an artefact of an
under-constrained asset block. With `K_s`, `L_s`, `age0` and `rho` sourced, the
support schedule solves to **`s_max = 198.1` at `lam = 0.0000`** — i.e. the
published €200 lump, flat, with no rationing. Fixing `s_max` at exactly 200 and
`lam` at exactly 0 and re-solving `gamma` alone reproduces rows 1–3 of the
ladder with **zero fitted support parameters**:

```
gamma   s_max     lam    → both targets hit
0.430   203.3   -0.0075
0.445   200.0    0.0000   ← adopted
0.450   198.1   -0.0000
0.500   185.3   +0.0191
```

No contradiction with §1.5 remains. The rationing mechanism is implemented and
available; it simply does not bind over the base-case decision window.

## What does not reproduce, and why

Of the 62 quantities the v11 specification publishes, **54 reproduce**. All
eight that do not are NPV *levels*, and all eight are high:

| §  | Item | Published | Computed | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| §3   | base case NPV | 1,588 | 1,606 | +1.1% |
| §4.2 | reform soft NPV | 1,155 | 1,247 | +8.0% |
| §4.2 | central NPV | 1,588 | 1,606 | +1.1% |
| §4.2 | tight market NPV | 2,339 | 2,370 | +1.3% |
| §4.2 | Booster in full NPV | 1,588 | 1,606 | +1.1% |
| §4.2 | rationed 30%/yr NPV | 1,570 | 1,604 | +2.2% |
| §4.4 | current law 2023/959 NPV | 1,581 | 1,606 | +1.6% |
| §4.4 | COM(2026) 616 NPV | 1,588 | 1,614 | +1.6% |

Not one commitment year, expiry year, trigger, crossing year, allocation factor
or input parameter differs. The model disagrees with the specification about
how much the decision is worth and agrees with it about when to take it — which
is the weaker of the two disagreements to have, since every published *decision*
survives. `analysis/reconcile.py` judges all eight against a single tolerance
(`NPV_TOL = 5`, the rounding on the published figures); an earlier version used
5 for the base case and 25 elsewhere, so the identical 1,588-vs-1,606
comparison showed as a pass in one section and a difference in another. A test
now pins one tolerance across all eight.

The two quantities that used to sit here have since closed:

| Item | Published | Computed | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| F8 vintage delta, `w=1` | +7.82 | +7.70 | eq (12) now read off the annex; was +8.97 |
| F8 vintage delta, `w=0` | −122 | −122.26 | reproduces; was −161 |

Row 4's *level* is right to €1; only the year-over-year slope differs enough to
move the argmax by one year on a curve that is flat to €8 across 2030–2032.

Rows 5–7 are the residual. At `a = −373` the NPV curve rises monotonically to
the horizon (−2751 in 2026, −49 in 2050), because the benefit flow only turns
positive around 2044 as the carbon price overtakes the hydrogen penalty. The
published −188 sits on that curve at ≈2044. Either the reference caps the
decision grid short of 2050, or it reports the value at a fixed year rather
than at the argmax. **`T*` = "beyond horizon" reproduces correctly** in every
one of these rows, which is the row's actual finding; only the accompanying
NPV level differs.

## Three places the spec's own text does not hold

1. **§1.7 eq (14) sanity check.** "As `sigma -> 0` the multiple -> 1 and the
   trigger collapses to break-even." That holds only when the drift is also
   zero. Under GBM with `mu > 0` the positive root tends to `r/mu`, so the
   multiple tends to `r/(r-mu)` = 2.667, not 1. Waiting is rewarded by the
   drift alone. Both limits are pinned by tests.

2. **§3.2, "never" at market volatility.** Correctly retracted in the spec, and
   this build confirms the retraction: at `sigma = 0.45` the trigger is €202.5
   and the path crosses it in **2043**. A test pins the crossing to 2040–2049
   so the wrong claim cannot come back.

3. **The eq (11) hand check conflates doublings with years.** Eq (2) is
   Wright's law over *doublings*: `2^(−b_ℓ·n)` with `b_ℓ = 0.2345`, giving
   0.614 at three doublings — that is where `I0 = 900` comes from
   (1465 × 0.614). The worked NPV in eq (11) then reuses **0.62 for a
   commitment three *years* out**, which would need deployed capacity to
   double every year. The model converts years to doublings through the
   deployment growth rate instead: three years at `gamma = 0.4442` is 1.59
   doublings, so `L(2029) = 0.773`, not 0.62. The companion says its own hand
   figure lands "within 4% of the code's 1,588", so the approximation is
   acknowledged there; a test pins the conversion so nobody corrects the model
   towards the arithmetic in the companion.

## Reconciliation status

The 50-case grid, the legacy-mode fixture and the diff harness are built and
tested, including the negative control (perturb one NPV by €5 and one `T*` by
one year → exactly two mismatches flagged). **The diff itself has not been
run**, because there is no MATLAB CSV to run it against. `run_cases()` produces
the Python side; drop the MATLAB CSV next to it and `diff()` completes.
