Read the unit before the number

Every euro figure in this model is per tonne of annual nameplate capacity — not per tonne of steel, and not a total. So capex I0 = €900 is EUR 2.2bn for a 2.5 Mt/yr works, and the NPV is in billions. Multiply by annual tonnes and the denominator cancels; that conversion is exact, not indicative.

A carbon price (€/tCO₂) and an operating flow (€/t of product per year) have different denominators and do not scale with plant size. Only the capacity-denominated stocks convert.

Stegra's Boden is 2.5 Mt/yr and cost €6.5bn to build. The published case says the switch alone is worth EUR 4.0bn on a modelled spend of EUR 2.2bn. Every project still under construction costs at least €1,300/t of capacity; the two below the model's €900 are an EAF-only retrofit and a scheme ArcelorMittal cancelled, forfeiting €1.3bn of approved German aid.

What is checked

  • The validation gate. Nine published quantities reproduce to 1e-3 relative before anything downstream prints.
  • Every table cell re-derived. 114 figures recomputed from the raw CSV cell or cached statistic — not from the function that prints them — and compared at the precision shown. 114/114 agree.
  • The specification, quantity by quantity. 54 of 62 published figures reproduce; all eight differences are NPV levels, high by 1.1–8.0%. No year, trigger or crossing year differs.
  • 290 tests and 41 standard-library self-checks, run on every build.

What is not

  • Provenance. Only 9 of 98 rows in the September 2026 research pass carry a working link. The rest name their source correctly without one — unverified, not sourced. None of it has been promoted into the running model.
  • Operating costs. The whole BF-BOF against H2-DRI-EAF difference is one constant driven by the hydrogen price. Real lines the model does not contain: DR-grade pellet premium, EAF electricity (€89/t in Germany against €47/t in Sweden), graphite electrodes, scrap and alloys. Closing this needs one study covering both routes at one boundary; none was found.
  • No tax layer. Electricity is dearer than gas per kWh in every member state, 1.8× to 3.9×, which is why a green hydrogen floor sits above a grey one. Those prices exclude taxes and levies, and the decomposition that “fiscal harmonisation” turns on was not obtained.
  • No MATLAB reference. The 50-case diff harness is built and its negative control works; there is no reference file to run it against.

The three choices the model will not make

  • Support basis. A capex lump or a per-tCO₂ stream. The German CCfD pays a stream over fifteen years, worth 1.3–3.3× the €200/t lump the model nets off.
  • Allowance resale. w = 1 drives the policy-neutrality result. Without it the headline reverses sign.
  • Whether €2.60/kg hydrogen is the base case at all. It sits below every green floor and inside the grey band — a fossil price carrying green emissions.
